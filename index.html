NutriStackiOS app, live on the App Store (v1.1)

A supplement tracker that checks your whole stack for dangerous combinations.

I designed, built and shipped NutriStack on my own. You add everything you take, and it screens the full stack for supplement and prescription interactions, schedules when to take each item, and tracks whether it’s helping. You can scan a bottle’s barcode or label to grade the product and see how it would change your stack. Its claims are backed by PubMed and DOI citations. The same knowledge base powers nutristackapp.com, a public research site with 1,142 indexed pages that brings in new users from search.

CourseTwin, private beta

An AI study workspace that won’t answer without a source.

Students record lectures or upload their own notes, and CourseTwin turns them into cited notes, practice questions and an exam study plan. The rule that shapes everything: if a claim can’t be traced to the student’s own material, it isn’t shown.

  • Click any citation in the notes and the lecture audio plays from that exact second.
  • Practice questions (multiple choice, multi-select, numeric and written) are solved independently and checked against the source before a student sees them.
  • An “Up next” study queue, one-click “Ask about this” on any note or passage, and a ⌘K bar to find or do anything.
  • Tracks mastery of each learning objective and builds an adaptive exam study plan.
  • Privacy first: an encrypted local database, no school-account integration, and recording only when the student starts it.

coursetwin: private beta

Prediction market arbitrage, research system

Can a $10K account profit from price gaps between Kalshi and Polymarket?

I built a system that collects order books from both venues, matches identical events across them, and simulates execution with real fees, 2–5 second latency and order-book depth. The first version streamed live prices over WebSockets from AWS EC2, and a collector still polls both venues every minute. Before testing each idea I wrote down what would count as a failure, then ran small real-money tests to check the simulator.

No strategy proved profitable, and I stopped each one when it hit a failure signal I’d set in advance. That discipline is the result I’m proudest of: testing an idea properly and killing it when the numbers say so.
